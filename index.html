Patrick Mutabazi

Authentication proves who logged in. I build security that proves what happens next: every high-impact action stays tied to the legitimate person and executes exactly what they approved.

My doctoral framework, Responsibility-Preserving Security, joins telecom-anchored Zero Trust identity, Actor Integrity (AIg) and Proof-of-Intent Authorization (PoIA) into one security chain. Each link is machine-checked with the Tamarin prover and tested in open research prototypes. It is built for the systems millions of people in Sub-Saharan Africa rely on: mobile money, internet banking and digital public services.

About

I’m a doctoral researcher at the Cyber Resilience Laboratory, Nara Institute of Science and Technology (NAIST), Japan. My path started in Rwanda, where I studied and worked on national cyber security and digital government services. That is where I saw how much daily life now depends on digital systems being trustworthy after sign-in.

My work joins theory and practice. I state security properties precisely, prove them with the Tamarin prover, and build prototypes with evaluations designed so they can report failure. Before my PhD I spent more than five years across industry and academia in security engineering, vulnerability assessment and penetration testing. That included consulting on Rwanda’s digital government services with GIZ and Irembo, and teaching network security at Carnegie Mellon University.

Research

Modern systems check credentials, enforce access policies and assess device posture, yet failures still happen after access succeeds. Credentials survive a SIM swap, sessions persist after control changes, and authorized requests execute something the person never approved. My dissertation treats identity, actor continuity and intent as one chain, and secures each link.

  1. Legitimacy

    Is this the legitimate account holder?

    In much of Sub-Saharan Africa the phone number is the identity, so a swapped SIM can hand an attacker someone’s savings. My telecom-anchored Zero Trust IAM uses operator signals to catch SIM swap and recovery reassignment before they take over an account. ZT-TOTP extends one-time passwords to resist phishing.

    SIM swap architecture, ICOIACT 2025. ZT-TOTP, part of the dissertation.

  2. Actor continuity

    Is it still the same person in control?

    Passing a login says nothing about who acts next. Actor Integrity (AIg) formalizes what it means for the same actor to remain in control of an authorized session. With collaborators, I also work on behavioural biometrics that confirm the account holder continuously in Tanzanian internet banking.

    AIg, under review at IJIS. KeystrokeMouse-TZ, APCC 2025. DABBiT, AAIML 2026.

  3. Intent integrity

    Is this exactly what they approved?

    Proof-of-Intent Authorization binds each protected execution to a single-use signed intent, with guarantees that run from commitment to execution (see PoIA below). Evaluated on banking and cloud-resource operations.

    PoIA, CSP 2026. Journal extension, in preparation.

Featured work, intent integrity

Proof-of-Intent Authorization (PoIA)

Enforcing intent integrity from commitment to execution

Malware does not need your password if it can act inside your session. PoIA lets a high-impact operation run only if it is exactly the one you approved.

A transaction generator steals nothing. It waits for a genuine login, then sends its own requests through the live session. Stronger authentication cannot see it, and trusted-device confirmation binds the signature only to what was displayed, not to what happens afterwards or to how that value was produced.

PoIA makes that binding an authorization condition. A protected operation executes only if a fresh, single-use proof over the relying party’s canonical intent is valid, and the executed request normalizes to the same semantics. The journal extension carries the guarantee across the whole lifecycle.

  1. Before signing

    k-of-n Independent Commitment Confinement

    At least k of n independent construction paths must agree on a value before it is shown and signed, so a single compromised source cannot decide what the person sees.

  2. At approval

    Intent Integrity

    The person signs a fresh, single-use proof over a canonical intent: the action, principal, scope, context and versioned referents. Execution must match it exactly.

  3. At execution

    Referent-State Integrity

    Referenced content, such as a stored beneficiary record, must still carry its committed version and digest, re-checked inside the transaction that performs the effect.

Together: Full-Lifecycle Non-Reconstitution. No execution can be assembled from an approval whose referent or commitment no longer holds.
